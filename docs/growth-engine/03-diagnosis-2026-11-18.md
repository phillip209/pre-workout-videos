## What the data says

1. **Pixel art fixed the opening.**
   - Stayed to watch went from 32–39 % (legacy) to 57–66 % (pixel, both formats).
   - Average view duration went from about 17 s to 21–30 s.
   - Scenario A (low stayed-to-watch) no longer describes the channel.
2. **The story format improved how much of the video people watch:** 66–68 % average viewed, against 49–54 %
   for the myth format at similar or longer duration. The alcohol video is the exception at 54 %.
3. **Reach did not change.**
   - Every Short is shown about 1.0–1.6 K times in the feed during the first hours, then distribution stops.
   - This holds regardless of stayed-to-watch (39 % → 66 %) or % viewed (42 % → 68 %).
   - This is **Scenario F: good engagement, limited reach.**
4. **Almost no returning audience:** 99 % new viewers, +1 to +5 subscribers per Short, near-zero comments.
   Retention is decent, but people do not come back for the channel, which points partly to Scenario E.

## Recommended controlled experiments (one variable each)

- **EXP-001 (H1, first 2 seconds):** next 2 Shorts with the same format and duration (~40 s). The variable is
  the first frame plus the first sentence, switching from an "intriguing statement" to "the outcome shown
  first". Metric: stayed to watch. Control: the story Shorts' 57–62 %.
- **EXP-002 (H4, duration):** one story cut to 20–25 s. Metrics: avg % viewed and reach. Control: 66–68 %
  viewed at ~43 s.
- **EXP-003 (H2, interaction):** a CTA that asks for a concrete, low-effort answer, such as "Escribe tu número
  de horas" or "¿A ti también te pasó?". Metric: comments per 1,000 views. Control: the current ~0.
